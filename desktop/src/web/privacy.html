Open Tiffin

Your data stays on your device.

Tiffin has no accounts, no cloud database, no analytics, no ads, and no tracking cookies. Your recipes, photos, meal plans, groceries, pantry, food diary, calorie targets, exercise logs, and body measurements live in your browser’s private storage (OPFS) on your device — never on a Tiffin server. Small display preferences (such as theme, layout, and last backup time) stay in local browser storage on the same device.

The only times anything leaves this device

Once loaded, Tiffin runs offline in WebAssembly. It makes an outside network request only when you trigger one of the four actions below:

1. Importing a recipe or nutrition page from a web address
Browsers block web apps from reading other websites directly (CORS). When you paste a recipe or product URL and ask Tiffin to import it, Tiffin sends only that web address to Tiffin’s stateless helper (helper.tiffin.fyi) to download the page HTML — and, if you save a recipe with a photo, to download that picture so it can be stored locally in your browser. The helper keeps no database, saves no files, and writes no logs of addresses or content.
2. Searching Open Food Facts by food name
Typing in Foods or Diary searches the bundled 18,000-food database completely offline. Nothing is sent while you type. Only when you press Search Online does Tiffin send your search text and the contact email you provided (in Settings or when first prompted, as Open Food Facts asks in its User-Agent) through the stateless helper to search.openfoodfacts.org.
3. Looking up a barcode on Open Food Facts
Decoding a barcode from a photo or your camera runs completely offline in WebAssembly on your device. In Foods and Diary, a scanned barcode checks your local foods and offline cache first, and contacts Open Food Facts only when it isn’t already saved locally. When an uncached scan looks up a product — or when you explicitly press Search Online or Look Up a barcode or Open Food Facts link on a food form — your browser asks world.openfoodfacts.org directly with your contact email in the X-User-Agent header, with no cookies and no referrer.
4. Opening an external link
Clicking a recipe source link, video link, or desktop download link opens that address in a new tab (noopener, noreferrer).

Camera and permissions

Tiffin asks for camera access only when you explicitly choose camera barcode scanning. Video frames are decoded locally in WebAssembly to read the barcode number and are never uploaded or recorded. Tiffin never requests your microphone, location, or other device sensors (screen wake lock is used only while Cook Mode is open so your screen stays awake).

Storage, backups, and deleting your data

  • Your browser holds your library: Recipes, diary entries, and automatic version snapshots stay in Origin Private File System (OPFS) storage on this device. Tiffin asks the browser to mark storage persistent so it isn’t cleared unexpectedly.
  • Portable one-file backups: In Settings → Backup & Restore, you can download everything as a single .recipebox file anytime, and restore it in another browser or in the Tiffin desktop app.
  • Delete all health data in one step: In Settings → Backup & Restore, Delete All Health Data removes your food diary, exercise logs, body measurements, profile, their snapshots, and health data inside in-browser backups (leaving your recipes and any .recipebox files you already downloaded intact).
  • Remove everything: Clear site data for tiffin.fyi in your browser’s site settings at any time to erase all local files, preferences, and offline caches.

Self-hosting and data sources

The helper server is a single stateless Rust program you can run on your own server if you prefer. Bundled nutrition data comes from USDA FoodData Central (public domain) and Health Canada’s Canadian Nutrient File (Open Government Licence — Canada); online product lookups come from Open Food Facts (ODbL).
